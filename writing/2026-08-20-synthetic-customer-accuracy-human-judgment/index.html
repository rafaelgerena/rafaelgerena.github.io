Synthetic Customer Accuracy Hits 90%: PwC and Bain Data Show Why Human Judgment Still Decides What to Trust

By Rafael Gerena · August 20, 2026

Summary: A PwC survey of senior executives found 79% report AI agents are already adopted in their companies, with 66% seeing measurable productivity value. At a recent ALPFA convention session, PwC's Max Harberg, Damon Hao, and Srivatsan Manikandan presented synthetic customers, AI-generated stand-ins for real customers, as a way to accelerate research. Bain & Company separately tested synthetic customer models against a major research study and matched about 90% of the original results, including which product features drove customer choice. Both firms reached the same conclusion: proprietary, company-specific data outperforms generic third-party data, and human research is still required to validate the highest-stakes decisions.

A PwC survey found 79% of executives say AI agents are already being adopted in their companies, with 66% reporting measurable value through higher productivity.

The findings were shared at the best session I attended at a recent ALPFA convention, "Faster, Smarter Decisions with Synthetic Customers." PwC's Max Harberg, Damon Hao, and Srivatsan Manikandan drew a standing-room-only crowd for a talk on synthetic customers: AI-generated stand-ins for real customers that can accelerate research without waiting on slow, traditional studies.

The session's framing matches what I've seen firsthand and in other research. Bain & Co. separately tested synthetic customer models against a major customer research study and matched about 90% of the original results, including which product features actually drove customer choice. Their conclusion, echoed by PwC's own team from the podium: original, company-specific data is higher quality and more reliable than generic third-party data. Relying on third-party data alone means working with weaker inputs that are harder to trust.

I learned the underlying discipline in earlier work. At a prior organization, conflicting analytics data during a website redesign did not clarify what users wanted. Rather than trust either signal, I sent a small team to interview a dozen users spanning different ages, backgrounds, and experience levels, concentrated in the exact area where the data disagreed. The interviews revealed a consistent desire: simpler navigation, cleaner typography, better contrast. The redesign that followed increased site usage by 75%.

PwC's central takeaway: use synthetic customers to explore quickly and broadly, then bring in human research to validate whatever you're about to bet real money on. The website redesign is that discipline in practice.

Synthetic data can replicate 90% of the truth, but deciding which 10% to distrust is a human judgment no model can make.
